How to read this

The tool separates two very different things.

Math is what the tool computes for you. You supply the facts — a salary, a number of months, a dollar amount — and every total, the F&A calculation, and the year-by-year rollup are worked out by code. No AI touches a number.

Checks are advice. The tool reads your finished budget and flags things NSF is likely to question. There are two levels, and neither one blocks you — you can always keep typing, save, and export:

  • Warning — a rule looks broken. Shown in red.
  • Notice — a requirement you have to satisfy somewhere else, usually in the written justification or a separate document. Shown in grey.

Part 2 — How the totals are worked out

Salary from person-months

NSF budgets effort in months, not percentages.

monthly rate = base salary / 9    (9-month academic appointment)
monthly rate = base salary / 12   (12-month calendar appointment)

salary = (CAL months + ACAD months + SUMR months) x monthly rate

Example: a $90,000 academic-year salary is $10,000/month. Two summer months = $20,000, which is 22% effort.

Fringe

Each person's fringe is their own rate times their own salary. Line C is the sum. A faculty member at 42% and a graduate student at 9% never share a rate.

The line rollup

H = A + B + C + D + E + F + G        total direct costs
I = MTDC x F&A rate                  indirect costs
J = H + I
K = fee                              (SBIR/STTR and Major Facilities only)
L = J - K                            the amount you are requesting

MTDC — the part most budgets get wrong

F&A is not charged on your whole budget. It is charged on the modified total direct costs:

MTDC = H
     - all equipment (line D)
     - all participant support (line F)
     - the part of EACH subaward over $25,000
     - any G-line item you tick as 'no F&A'

That last line is the tuition case. NSF's form has no tuition box, and Morgan books graduate tuition remission in G.6 Other, sitting right next to items that do carry F&A. The tool cannot tell $40,000 of tuition from $40,000 of lab fees by looking at them — and guessing wrong moves your total by about $21,600 at the 54% rate. So you tick a box, and the math follows. The same box covers scholarships, fellowships, rent, and patient care.

Multiple years

Each year is its own sheet. Add year copies the previous year and raises salaries only by 3% — equipment, travel and subawards carry over unchanged, because quietly inflating those is how a budget grows without anyone noticing. The Cumulative sheet is added up fresh every time you look at it and is never stored, so it cannot fall out of step with the years.

Part 4 — What the tool does not know

Two limits worth stating plainly, because a tool that hides them is worse than one that admits them.

1. The two-month cap counts across every NSF award you hold. This tool only sees the proposal in front of it. It will catch you budgeting three summer months here, but it has no idea what you have committed on your other NSF grants. That total is yours to track.

2. Morgan's equipment capitalization level is unconfirmed. The tool defaults to $5,000, which is what PAPPG specifies when an organization's own level is not lower. If ORA capitalizes at a different figure, changing that one setting reclassifies equipment correctly everywhere. Note that Form 1030's line D says $10,000 — that is only about which items you list by name on the form, and is a different thing from the definition that decides whether F&A applies.

Also worth confirming: the F&A and fringe rates above are read from the knowledge base, not from ORA's live rate agreement. Worth a sanity check against ORA's current rate sheet before any of this goes on a real submission.
