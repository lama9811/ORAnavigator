Project Summary

Overview
This project (LOI #2345678) develops adaptive zwitterionic polymer networks for continuous salinity sensing in estuarine waters. The objectives are to synthesize antifouling zwitterionic hydrogels, characterize their conductance response across 0-35 PSU, and validate deployed prototypes in the Chesapeake Bay over twelve months. The methods combine controlled radical polymerization, impedance spectroscopy, and field calibration against reference CTD instruments.

Intellectual Merit
The work advances fundamental understanding of ion partitioning in zwitterionic networks, a regime where existing Donnan models fail. It will produce the first quantitative structure-response relationship linking charge density to salinity sensitivity.

Broader Impacts
The project trains four undergraduates and two graduate students per year at Morgan State University, an HBCU. Sensor data will be shared with the Maryland Department of the Environment, and a summer module will reach thirty Baltimore City high school students each year.

Project Description

1. Introduction and Objectives
This project addresses the lack of low-drift, antifouling salinity sensors for long-term estuarine deployment. The expected significance is a sensor platform that holds calibration for twelve months where current instruments drift within six weeks.

2. Relationship to the State of Knowledge
Existing conductivity sensors rely on platinum electrodes that biofoul. Zwitterionic coatings are known to resist protein adsorption but their ion-transport behaviour under variable salinity is uncharacterized. The PI's prior work under internal Morgan State funding established the synthesis route used here.

3. General Plan of Work
Aim 1: Synthesize zwitterionic hydrogel networks with controlled charge density using RAFT polymerization.
Aim 2: Characterize conductance and impedance response across 0-35 PSU and 5-35 C using a Gamry potentiostat.
Aim 3: Deploy six prototypes at Chesapeake Bay stations and calibrate against reference CTD instruments over twelve months.

4. Results from Prior NSF Support
No prior NSF support.

Broader Impacts
Four undergraduates and two graduate students per year at Morgan State University, an HBCU, will be trained in materials characterization and field instrumentation. A summer module will reach thirty Baltimore City high school students each year, and all sensor data will be released publicly.

Budget and Budget Justification

Senior personnel: the PI requests one summer month per year (1.0 person-month) to lead synthesis and supervise students, at an annual base salary of $95,000. For the purposes of this budget, "year" means the twelve-month period beginning on the award start date.

Other personnel: two graduate research assistants at 12 months each at $28,000 per year. One postdoctoral associate, 1 FTE, 12 person-months per year, at $58,000 per year.

Equipment: a Gamry Interface 1010E potentiostat, $18,400, required for the impedance measurements in Aim 2. No comparable instrument is available at Morgan State.

Travel: one domestic conference per year for the PI and one student ($2,400/yr), plus the annual NSF grantee meeting in Washington, DC ($1,200/yr).

Participant support: fifteen high school participants in the summer module, at $500 stipend each.

Materials and supplies: monomers, chain transfer agents, sensor housings and consumables, $18,000 per year.

Indirect costs are calculated at Morgan State's federally negotiated rate of 51% of modified total direct costs.

Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources

The Department of Chemistry at Morgan State University provides 1,200 square feet of wet laboratory space dedicated to this project, including two fume hoods and a controlled-humidity room. Available equipment includes a Bruker 400 MHz NMR, a Gamry potentiostat for impedance spectroscopy, and a Malvern light scattering instrument. Field access to Chesapeake Bay monitoring stations is provided through an existing agreement with the Maryland Department of Natural Resources. Dr. Elena Ruiz of the Maryland Department of the Environment will advise on deployment siting; no funds are requested for her participation. Computing support is provided by the University's HPC cluster.

Data Management and Sharing Plan

All calibration and field time-series data will be released in NetCDF format through the Chesapeake Bay Program data portal within twelve months of collection. Synthesis protocols and characterization spectra will be deposited in the Materials Data Facility. Data will be preserved for a minimum of ten years.

Postdoctoral Mentoring Plan

The postdoctoral associate will receive structured mentoring in proposal writing, laboratory management, and undergraduate supervision, with quarterly individual development plan reviews and support to attend one national conference per year.
