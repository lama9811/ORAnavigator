Letter of Intent

Adaptive Zwitterionic Networks for Coastal Salinity Sensing

PI: Dr. A. Researcher, Morgan State University.

We propose to develop antifouling salinity sensors for the Chesapeake Bay.

Project Summary

Overview
This project develops adaptive zwitterionic polymer networks for continuous salinity sensing in estuarine waters. The objectives are to synthesize antifouling hydrogels, characterize their conductance response, and validate prototypes in the Chesapeake Bay.

Intellectual Merit
The work advances understanding of ion partitioning in zwitterionic networks, a regime where existing Donnan models fail.

Broader Impacts
The project trains undergraduate and graduate students at Morgan State University and shares sensor data with the Maryland Department of the Environment.

Project Description

1. Background
Existing conductivity sensors rely on platinum electrodes that biofoul within six weeks of estuarine deployment. Zwitterionic coatings resist protein adsorption.

2. Research Plan
Aim 1: Synthesize zwitterionic hydrogel networks with controlled charge density using RAFT polymerization.
Aim 2: Characterize conductance and impedance response across 0-35 PSU.
Aim 3: Deploy six prototypes at Chesapeake Bay stations.

3. Preliminary Data
Initial syntheses produced three candidate networks with measured swelling ratios between 4.2 and 7.8.

Broader Impacts
Four undergraduates and two graduate students per year will be trained in materials characterization. A summer module will reach thirty Baltimore City high school students each year.

Budget and Budget Justification

Senior personnel: the PI requests one summer month per year at an annual base salary of $95,000.

Other personnel: two graduate research assistants at 12 months each at $28,000 per year.

Equipment: a Gamry Interface 1010E potentiostat ($18,400), a Malvern Zetasizer ($64,000), and an environmental chamber ($41,000), totalling $123,400 of the $290,000 total project budget.

Travel: two domestic conferences per year for the PI and one student ($4,800/yr).

Materials and supplies: monomers, chain transfer agents and consumables, $18,000 per year.

Collaboration: Dr. R. Alvarez at Coastal State University will provide field deployment support. We have budgeted $40,000 for his effort as a consultant.

Morgan State University will contribute $25,000 in voluntary committed cost sharing to demonstrate institutional support.

Indirect costs are calculated at Morgan State's federally negotiated rate of 51% of modified total direct costs.
