Project Summary

Overview
This project (LOI #2345678) develops adaptive zwitterionic polymer networks for continuous salinity sensing in estuarine waters. The objectives are to synthesize antifouling zwitterionic hydrogels, characterize their conductance response across 0-35 PSU, and validate deployed prototypes in the Chesapeake Bay over twelve months. The methods combine controlled radical polymerization, impedance spectroscopy, and field calibration against reference CTD instruments.

Intellectual Merit
The work advances fundamental understanding of ion partitioning in zwitterionic networks, a regime where existing Donnan models fail. It will produce the first quantitative structure-response relationship linking charge density to salinity sensitivity, enabling rational design of low-drift sensors rather than empirical selection.

Broader Impacts
The project trains four undergraduates and two graduate students per year at Morgan State University, an HBCU, in materials characterization and field instrumentation. Sensor data will be shared with the Maryland Department of the Environment, and a summer module will reach thirty Baltimore City high school students each year.

Project Description

1. Overall Research Goals and How This Project Advances Them
The PI's long-term research goal is to establish structure-property design rules for zwitterionic materials in complex ionic environments. This project advances that goal by resolving the ion-partitioning regime in which existing models fail, and it positions the PI to compete for sustained federal support in polymer materials beyond this award.

2. Institutional Context
Morgan State University is Maryland's preeminent public urban research university and an accredited HBCU. The Department of Chemistry currently has no dedicated impedance spectroscopy capability, and faculty seeking such measurements travel to partner institutions. This project establishes that capacity in-house.

3. Background and Literature
Existing conductivity sensors rely on platinum electrodes that biofoul within six weeks of estuarine deployment (Chen 2019). Zwitterionic coatings are known to resist protein adsorption (Smith and Lee 2021), but their ion-transport behaviour under variable salinity is uncharacterized; Donnan partitioning models break down above 20 PSU (Alvarez et al. 2020).

4. Research Questions and Hypotheses
Q1: How does zwitterion charge density govern conductance sensitivity across 0-35 PSU? We hypothesize a monotonic relationship that saturates above a critical charge density.
Q2: Does antifouling performance trade off against ion transport? We hypothesize the two are separable through network architecture rather than chemistry.

5. Preliminary Data
Initial syntheses produced three candidate networks with measured swelling ratios between 4.2 and 7.8, and preliminary impedance sweeps show a 40% conductance shift across 5-25 PSU, supporting the feasibility of Aim 2.

6. Experimental Strategy and Timeline
Aim 1 (Months 1-12): Synthesize networks with controlled charge density using RAFT polymerization.
Aim 2 (Months 9-24): Characterize conductance and impedance across 0-35 PSU and 5-35 C.
Aim 3 (Months 18-36): Deploy six prototypes at Chesapeake Bay stations and calibrate against reference CTD instruments.

7. Improving Student Research Opportunities
Four undergraduates and two graduate students per year will be trained in polymer synthesis, impedance spectroscopy and field instrumentation. Each undergraduate completes a full research cycle culminating in a presentation at the Morgan State research symposium.

8. Improving Institutional Research Capacity
The award establishes a shared polymer characterization facility open to all Morgan State faculty, staffed by a technician at 25% effort. The instrument and protocols remain after the award and are governed by a departmental access policy already drafted.

9. Significant Collaborative Arrangements
Dr. R. Alvarez at Coastal State University will provide access to Chesapeake Bay monitoring stations and field deployment support, structured as a subaward.

10. Sustainability Plan
Beyond the award period, the shared characterization facility will be sustained through a recharge model already approved by the Dean, at rates benchmarked against regional peer institutions. The PI will submit a follow-on proposal to the NSF Polymers Program in Year 3, and the departmental access policy commits ongoing technician support from state funds.

11. Metrics for Project Success
Success will be measured by: sensors holding calibration within 5% over twelve months; at least four peer-reviewed publications; six students trained per year with at least two continuing to graduate study; and the shared facility recording at least 200 instrument-hours per year by non-project faculty.

12. Scholarly Dissemination Plan
Results will be published in Macromolecules and ACS Sensors, presented annually at the ACS National Meeting and the Ocean Sciences Meeting, and all calibration data released through the Chesapeake Bay Program data portal.

Broader Impacts
Four undergraduates and two graduate students per year at Morgan State University, an HBCU, will be trained in materials characterization and field instrumentation. A summer module will reach thirty Baltimore City high school students each year, and all sensor data will be released publicly to the Maryland Department of the Environment.

Budget and Budget Justification

Total project budget: $290,000 over three years.

Senior personnel: the PI requests one summer month per year (1.0 person-month) at an annual base salary of $95,000. For the purposes of this budget, "year" means the twelve-month period beginning on the award start date.

Other personnel: two graduate research assistants at 12 months each at $28,000 per year. One technician at 25% effort to operate the shared characterization facility.

Equipment: a Gamry Interface 1010E potentiostat, $18,400, required for the impedance measurements in Aim 2. Equipment is $18,400 of the $290,000 total, which is 6.3% and well within the 30% limit this solicitation sets.

Travel: one domestic conference per year for the PI and one student ($2,400/yr), plus travel to the annual NSF HBCU-EiR grantee meeting in Washington, DC, budgeted at $1,200 per year for the PI.

Subaward: Coastal State University, a non-HBCU partner, receives a subaward of $42,000 across three years for field deployment support. This is 14.5% of the total budget. Their scope of work and separate budget justification are attached. The subaward is structured as a subaward rather than a consultant arrangement because the work is a substantive programmatic collaboration.

Materials and supplies: monomers, chain transfer agents, sensor housings and consumables, $18,000 per year.

No voluntary committed cost sharing is included in this budget.

Indirect costs are calculated at Morgan State's federally negotiated rate of 51% of modified total direct costs.
