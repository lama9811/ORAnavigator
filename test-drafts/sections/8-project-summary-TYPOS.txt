Overview
This project (LOI #2345678) develops adaptive zwiterionic polymer networks for continuous salinity sensing in estuarine waters. The objectives is to synthesise antifouling zwitterionic hydrogels, characterize their conductance response across 0-35 PSU, and validate deployed prototypes in the the Chesepeake Bay over twelve months. The methods combine controlled radical polymerization, impedance spectroscopy, and field calibration against reference CTD instruments

Intellectual Merit
The work advance fundamental understanding of ion partitioning in zwitterionic networks, a regime where existing Donnan models fails. It will produce the first quantitative structure-response relationship linking charge density to salinity sensitivity, enabling rational design of low-drift sensors rather then empirical selection.

Broader Impacts
The project train four undergraduates and two graduate students per year at Morgan State Univeristy, an HBCU, in materials characterization and field instrumentation. Sensor data will be shared with the Maryland Departement of the Environment, and a summer module will be delivered to Baltimore City high school students.
